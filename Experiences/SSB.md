# SSB Interview Experience

This blog is about my ssb experience which happened between 4th Oct 2026 (Sunday) to 8th Oct (Thursday). It was 5 days long. Let me first very briefly go through the entire process, then talk about my opinions on my performance and why I didn't get selected, and finally a few words of disappointment.

The tests on the first day were two mental ability apitude kind of tests, which were both really easy. After which there was something called PPDT (picture perception and description test). In this test, there was a black and white image in which, in my perception was a lady standing, thanking the man who had found her lost baby. Others did differently. At the end, we were supposed to narrate individually our stories and by discussing had to come up with a common story for the image. Our group couldn't, thanks to one of the very dominating, coached guy, who did actually get recommended later. In the PPDT test he continued to speak, and after a while all of my team-mates made it a fish market. We couldn't come to a consensus. That was all for the tests; we were taken to fill a few forms and that's was all for the day.

The second day was Psychology test. I think the reason I did not get selected is this particular day by the most degree. I believe I came across as way too ambitious. I wrote everything about wanting to be respected and known and "famous". This is exactly what I had written. I must not have. I think I am too good for the military that they didn't take me. This miss-alignment with the military requirments was most seen by the accessors in the Psych test. I wrote everthing about starting a club etc. that they thought of me as more on the entrepreneur side, which is not wrong, but I can give up my abitions for military now, really, I have become passionate about military after these 5 days spent in SSB centre. "Itna din rahoonga toh man ho hi jayega na". Read about the tests on the internet if you are interested but it was the reason I didn't get selected.

The third  day was the GTO-1, followed by interviews of a few people. I had my interview on this day. The GTO, who looked like a MARCO, was wearing dark shades. We discussed his startegy among ourselves - "he's wearing classes to not show where he is looking; he's pointing his head there, but his eyesight is elsewhere!". We had a group discussion on two topics, "AI" and "why students have more career opportunities than in the past". I spoke 3 points in either and when I spoke I made sure everyone was quiet. My points were banger and very clear and elite and premium. It must have impressed the GTO. "With time the number of ideas in society increase, but earlier it was difficult to pursue them, now with internet it has become easier, thus career opportunities have increased", was one of my points; isn't it elite and premium. "With the boom of AI, the semi-conductor industry has boomed too; tomorrow a new industry comes and not just booms itself but helps grow other industries - meaning, with time as industries grow they make other industries grow too, and thus the opporunities increase exponentially", was another point I had said. So, overall the GD (group discussion) was elite for me. After which we had GPE (Group Planning Exercise), in which we were given a situation which we had to reallistically solve. We were told to write our individual answers first in a paper, then there was a group discussion which was to come to a common consensus. In this discussion, our team-mates were shouting like in fish markets; I stopped shouting, and started listening to everyone's points and jotted every answer, and made a common answer. Had I not been there, they would have not made a common answer. I take a credit for this. They must thank me. Everyone thinks, they must speak so the GTO looks at them and gives them marks, but in the discussion in GPE, we were to come to a common consensus; the GTO also stopped us twice to tell us that this way we may not be able to come to a common consensus, but the my team-mates would again start like a fish-market. At the end, I raised my hand to present, for which everyone hated me. But they wouldn't be able to do it without me. After these exercises, we were taken to a snake-race in which we had to take a folded tent, kind of like a long cylinder and 9-team-mates in out team, would have to together negotiate with the obstacles and finish. We finished first, but we weren't saying our war-cry out loud so after the finishing our GTO told us to re-do the last obstacle and we came second due to it. I was confident until now, but henceforth were the group taks. This was a mess. We couldn't do very well here. After this, individual obstacle exercise, in which in 3 minutes we had to do 10 obstacles. By memory, I think I did 5-6 obstacles out of 10 in 3 minutes. I didn't do this very well. But the obstacle in which one has to climb up a rope, I did it but took too much time. I used all my power to do it, that I am proud of. That was all for this day.
I had my interview on the third day after GTO-1. I was called in the waiting room at 1pm after lunch, the line was long, so I skipped the lunch for the interview, which eventually happend at 3.30pm and ended at 4.30pm, about an hour. My interview started great (10/10), ended not so great (7/10). I came across as too dull and a boring person to the interviewer. However my opinions about the Indian Navy were spot on and my answers, again, elite and premium. "Army is a more labor-intensive force, navy is more capital-intensive. Navy needs smaller crews but highly speciallized that manage millions of dollars of equipments. The ratio, Naval Tonnage by Number of Active Personal is higher of India, than even China and America, meaning we manage a long coastline and more tonnage with a relatively smaller force - meaning - we are more efficient." This was my answer. This must have impressed the interviewer. But when he asked me about university, I answered with too much ambitions and when he asked if I'd go back to home from hostel on holidays, I said I would not. Which I think he may not have liked. We had a discussion on books I had read - I read a lot of books. So this was fine. At the end he asked me some news and concluded the interview.

The fourth day, was GTO-2, which for me was fantastic.

The fifth day, was conference. I did not feel nervous AT ALL in front of the 18-20 (whatever) number of officers were sitting. I even made them laugh by saying, "if i get a chance, i'll stay here indefinetly".
