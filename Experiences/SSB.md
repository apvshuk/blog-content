# SSB Interview Experience

This blog is about my ssb experience which happened between 4th Oct 2026 (Sunday) to 8th Oct (Thursday). It was 5 days long. Let me first very briefly go through the entire process, then talk about my opinions on my performance and why I didn't get selected, and finally a few words of disappointment.

**Day2**: The second day was Psychology test. I think the reason I did not get selected is this particular day by the most degree. I believe I came across as way too ambitious. I wrote everything about wanting to be respected and known and "famous". This is exactly what I had written. I must not have. I think I am too good for the military that they didn't take me. This miss-alignment with the military requirments was most seen by the accessors in the Psych test. I wrote everthing about starting a club etc. that they thought of me as more on the entrepreneur side, which is not wrong, but I can give up my abitions for military now, really, I have become passionate about military after these 5 days spent in SSB centre. "Itna din rahoonga toh man ho hi jayega na". Read about the tests on the internet if you are interested but it was the reason I didn't get selected.

**Day3**: The third  day was the GTO-1, followed by interviews of a few people. I had my interview on this day. The GTO, who looked like a MARCO, was wearing dark shades. We discussed his startegy among ourselves - "he's wearing classes to not show where he is looking; he's pointing his head there, but his eyesight is elsewhere!". We had a group discussion on two topics, "AI" and "why students have more career opportunities than in the past". I spoke 3 points in either and when I spoke I made sure everyone was quiet. My points were banger and very clear and elite and premium. It must have impressed the GTO. "With time the number of ideas in society increase, but earlier it was difficult to pursue them, now with internet it has become easier, thus career opportunities have increased", was one of my points; isn't it elite and premium. "With the boom of AI, the semi-conductor industry has boomed too; tomorrow a new industry comes and not just booms itself but helps grow other industries - meaning, with time as industries grow they make other industries grow too, and thus the opporunities increase exponentially", was another point I had said. So, overall the GD (group discussion) was elite for me. After which we had GPE (Group Planning Exercise), in which we were given a situation which we had to reallistically solve. We were told to write our individual answers first in a paper, then there was a group discussion which was to come to a common consensus. In this discussion, our team-mates were shouting like in fish markets; I stopped shouting, and started listening to everyone's points and jotted every answer, and made a common answer. Had I not been there, they would have not made a common answer. I take a credit for this. They must thank me. Everyone thinks, they must speak so the GTO looks at them and gives them marks, but in the discussion in GPE, we were to come to a common consensus; the GTO also stopped us twice to tell us that this way we may not be able to come to a common consensus, but the my team-mates would again start like a fish-market. At the end, I raised my hand to present, for which everyone hated me. But they wouldn't be able to do it without me. After these exercises, we were taken to a snake-race in which we had to take a folded tent, kind of like a long cylinder and 9-team-mates in out team, would have to together negotiate with the obstacles and finish. We finished first, but we weren't saying our war-cry out loud so after the finishing our GTO told us to re-do the last obstacle and we came second due to it. I was confident until now, but henceforth were the group taks. This was a mess. We couldn't do very well here. After this, individual obstacle exercise, in which in 3 minutes we had to do 10 obstacles. By memory, I think I did 5-6 obstacles out of 10 in 3 minutes. I didn't do this very well. But the obstacle in which one has to climb up a rope, I did it but took too much time. I used all my power to do it, that I am proud of. That was all for this day.
I had my interview on the third day after GTO-1. I was called in the waiting room at 1pm after lunch, the line was long, so I skipped the lunch for the interview, which eventually happend at 3.30pm and ended at 4.30pm, about an hour. My interview started great (10/10), ended not so great (7/10). I came across as too dull and a boring person to the interviewer. However my opinions about the Indian Navy were spot on and my answers, again, elite and premium. "Army is a more labor-intensive force, navy is more capital-intensive. Navy needs smaller crews but highly speciallized that manage millions of dollars of equipments. The ratio, Naval Tonnage by Number of Active Personal is higher of India, than even China and America, meaning we manage a long coastline and more tonnage with a relatively smaller force - meaning - we are more efficient." This was my answer. This must have impressed the interviewer. But when he asked me about university, I answered with too much ambitions and when he asked if I'd go back to home from hostel on holidays, I said I would not. Which I think he may not have liked. We had a discussion on books I had read - I read a lot of books. So this was fine. At the end he asked me some news and concluded the interview.

**Day4**: The fourth day, was GTO-2, which for me was fantastic. The first task was a lecturette on the spot - meaning, a topic be given, and one has to speak with a minimal preparation. I spoke on - "My Favourite memory". I spoke decently. Then there was what is called The Command Task (CT). I did excellent in this. Each candidate is called one by one. They are briefly interviewd, casually, by the GTO. My GTO was interested in me it seemed, for he spoke to me for quite some time. He asked me about my father and much, I told him about Shukla Index too. The GTO of the other team also started talking to me. They asked me the full form of RADAR - Radio Detection and Ranging.
Then the CT started; I was told, I am on an island which is sinking very fast and I must get out of it quickly using only the materials provided. For help, I can call two others from the team but they can't talk, but only follow my commands - he said I am the commander and they the sub-ordinates; that's how the CT was structured. Now, he did not tell me which direction is which, but I must get out from the western direction. Using the sun, I could find the western direction - that I think impressed him. Then I solved the problem using all 4 helping materials provided - balli, plank, rope and a metal rail. GTO said, "suppose in the hurricane the balli is lost - how do you get out?" I did it now without the balli - that's the second solution I could give. Then he said, the rope is gone too. I did it without it - I made a canti-lever with the balli and the metal rail (the metal rail was un-negotiable, it had to be used - it had to be inserted in a grove). So I gave 3 solutions + I found the direction without him telling me, using the sun. This certainly impressed me. The fact that he tested me with increasingly difficult situations implies he must have recommended me. I am sure.
After this there was a Final Group Task - which we did superbly - we did the challenge in way less than 10 minutes, which was the time limit. And this one was really great team spirit. Meaning, we had learnt to work as a team by the time the final test had arrived - which is a good thing. In all the team tasks, I did not show desparation to perform, I watched, I helped, but I did not do things to show that I am desparate for the GTO officer to look at me. I was Insouciance (carefree).

The fifth day, was conference. I did not feel nervous AT ALL in front of the 18-20 (whatever) number of officers were sitting. I even made them laugh by saying, "if i get a chance, i'll stay here indefinetly".
