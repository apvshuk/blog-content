# Frankenstein By Mary Shelly
I read this book in 2025. The one part that I re-read everytime I chance upon this book on my shelf, is chapter four. As Napoleon says, read and re-read the books that you think define your life. Re-reading was important to Napoleon. Not all books deserve re-reading, some do. More on that in some hisory blog.
Chapter four is about Frankenstein completely giving himself to sciences of his interest and the university curriculum, which motivates me so much.

> From this day natural philosophy, and particularly chemistry, in the most comprehensive sense of the term, became nearly my sole occupation. I read with ardour those works, so full of genius and discrimination, which modern inquirers have written on these subjects. I attended the lectures and cultivated the acquaintance of the men of science of the university...

That is my favourite quote from the entire book. It is the first line of chapter 4.

The reader knows at one point that Frankenstein has spent two years occupied with his curriculum that he paid no visit to his hometown back in Geneva.
Geneva is a city in the French speaking part of Switzerland. So Frankenstein must have spoken French with his family.

A great detail by Mary Shelly:
Frankenstein goes to University of Ingolstadt. This university, was infamously associated with the feared sect of conspirators known as the 'Illuminati', in the 18th century. Also, this university was thought by some to be formenters of the French Revolution.
So it has had a reputation of a certain kind, and Mary Shelly using it in her book as the place where Frankenstein, later, creates a new being is not a bad easter egg, sort of.


That makes me think of Mary Shelly. She was very young when she published the book, and possibly even younger when she began to write the book, which is still remembered after 200 years. She published anonymously at first, for women were not taken seriously as writers. She proved it wrong. She was maybe 19 years old when she published. That's why I admire her. Young, woman in an era discouraging for women, but wrote a book remembered for 200 years. There's another woman in history I admire - Marie Antoinette, more on her in another blog.

The book mentions, indirectly the phase where Frankenstein begins to specialize in a sub-domain of science - physiology.
He studies ardently for two years, not knowing when sun rised and when it sets, then he begins physiology, and again starts that phase where he knows nothing about the outside world. He locks himself for days in his room and works. Like Newton says, lock yourself, and study until you collapse; true brilliance comes from obsession. I don't know if he said that, but I read somewhere this quote associated to him.

There's another quote in Frankenstein which I like, and it quite fits "Kafkaesque":
> ...tortured the living animal to animate the lifeless clay...
That is Frankenstein narrating his plight to Robert Walton (or whatever his name was, he is actually my more favourite character in this book), about him killing and torturing living animals to create new life; how ironic. This qoute actually shows how obsessiveness can be so ironic yet tempting and to-die-for.
