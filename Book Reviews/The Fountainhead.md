# The Fountainhead

This is particularly a special book for me; and for a special reason. When I write these articles I usually don't have the book in front of me; this is to encourage my mind to recall the things I am most ardent about. Here is a quote I really love from the book. So let me quot from my memory something from the book which made me go bonkers when I had read it the first time:
> Dean: "...do you really plan to made such buildings son?"
> Roark: "Yes"
> Dean: "Who will let you?"
> Roark: "That's not the point. The point is, who will stop me!"

This interview of Roark with the Dean is the best part of the book, and it is (i don't remember exactly), maybe in the first 50 pages of the book. So you can start reading the book and you'll reach this part in no time.

There's another quote, from the same interview of Roark with the Dean, that I will quote from memory again:
> Roark: "You see those people over there; I don't give a damn about what they think about architecture, why should I care what there grandfather's though about architecture."
Roark says this to the dean. The dean and the college had been trying to persuade Roark to follow the college curriculum which Roark thought is redundant. The college curriculum pastes everything that the greeks did, but anything new. Roark dislikes this. He likes originality. Every college assignment he gets, he ignores the instructions and designs something modern devoid of anything that the greeks did. He does not want to copy the greeks or the egyptians etc. He wants to build new. This the college dislikes, and so finally he is being rusticated. This is the interview the dean takes to give Roark a final chance, in which he still refuses to conform, and is rusticated. He accepts it finally with no emotions whatsoever. He says those words to illustrate that he doesn't care about what the past did in architecture. He wants to build for the present.

He is a modernist one may say. He is an originalist I say. I want to be like him too.
Although there are certain things about him I don't like.

For example, he is not social. I want to be social. I want to be a leader. Who do you want to be a leader of? - People! But people are not individuals. They are people. To be a leader one has to take everyone together. Howard Roark would be a bad leader - he is an individualist.
Steve Jobs had said something like - Building is like climbing a mountain. Now when you climb a mountain, you have an assortment of things. You need people to carry those. One cannot do it alone, however great individual he may be.... Something like that. Roark is completely not this. He will blast a building but not conform.

He is unyielding; although it could be a compliment, it is not in the case of Roark. I want to be unyielding in the sense that I don't give up on a task. Unyielding is bad, when one is unyielding in his beliefs and cannot bear others. That's bad. Although Roark does bear other people - or does he simply ignore them. He doesn't care.

I want to adopt the concept of individualism with a pinch of conformism. I will conform for others, if they are nice to me. Although I do feel sometimes that I have the rudeness of Roark towards people. But I am usually nice.

Now back to the book.
