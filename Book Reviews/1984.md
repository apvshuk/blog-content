# 1984 by George Orwell

This is possibly the only book which almost made me cry at the end of the book.
The last paragraph actually takes the entire book and condenses it into one emotion, of sorrow and defeat.
Reading the book one hopes for a happy ending; this book breaks that.
While reading I did think that the Smith will somehow with Julia escape the dystopia and be free in another country with
the rebels, and live happily ever after. I did initially suspect Julia to be honey trapping Smith, but with time I believed her too.
I never suspected O'Brien, never ever. That was how George Orwell played with my emotions. But the overall experience taught me what great story telling is.
1984 plays with your emotions; I believe a story or a film capable of doing that is a great story or film.
That part of the book where Julia and Smith are set free after the torture and are sitting together, not talking to each other, not interested in each other
is really heartbreaking for the reader; the torture was so brutal that they stopped loving - that is sad. It was so brutal that Winston wanted to escape by passing the torture
to Julia, that's what the torture did. That's the sad part. At the end when Winston is sitting in the shop and watches Big Brother, tears roll down his cheek and he loves Big Brother:

> “He gazed up at the enormous face. Forty years it had taken him to learn what kind of smile was hidden beneath the dark moustache. O cruel, needless misunderstanding! O stubborn, self-willed exile from the loving breast! Two gin-scented tears trickled down the sides of his nose. But it was all right, everything was all right, the struggle was finished. He had won the victory over himself. He loved Big Brother.”

This is very sad, because in the beginning of the book, there were 3 people of the same tendency, mentally dead, hope lost, no life left. In the beginning the reader overlooks that.
But reading the last paragraph connects to those 3 people and tells the reader how exactly they were feeling then. This made me feel so sad.
The entire book gives the reader a hope, the end shatters that hope.

While reading the book, I did feel the dystopia myself, I was feeling the desoleteness. I could feel the emptyness in the lives of the people. There was no life in such a world.
It is hollow and empty and sad and lonely - nobody is nobody's friend - having no friends is so lonely. Thus I could feel the horror of the dystopia, but the hope of a happy ending
made me still positive, but Orwell shattered it with the end.

I remember completing the book in about 10 days - 300 pages, 10 days. It is a short book and I read it in a short period of time. It is my theory that the more time you spend with a book, the more it hits at the end, and the more you miss it after it ends. For example, Crime and Punishement or Fountainhead, or big books; one can't help but take some significant peiod of time
to finish them. So the endings hit. However if a short book, finished in a short period hits as much, it signifies the brilliance of the book. I conclude by this theory of mine, that
1984 is a brilliant piece of storytelling, and that I must re-read and re-read it multiple times to be able to extract elements of great storytelling out of it.
